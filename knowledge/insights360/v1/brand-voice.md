# Insights360 — Brand Voice

> **For the campaign team:** Read this end-to-end before drafting any Insights360
> content. Two-or-three-rule quotes go into briefs; the full document
> is the calibration target. When voice rules and product copy from
> the website conflict, this document wins.

## The one-line description

Insights360 is Conversant Technologies' multi-tenant contact-centre
intelligence platform. Enterprise-grade QA, agent performance, risk,
and sentiment analytics — at a price that doesn't require a 50-seat
minimum. Compatible with the major telephony platforms (Microsoft
Teams, 3CX, Cisco, Avaya, Mitel, 8x8, Five9, RingCentral, OAK/ClarifyGo)
— it ingests the recordings those platforms produce. Post-call,
fully automated. Score 100% of calls instead of the 2–5% manual QA
catches. (Compatibility ≠ automated feed — see "Canonical figures".)

Website tagline: **"See every conversation. Improve every outcome."**
Sub: **"Turn every customer conversation into actionable insight,
automatically, no matter which telephony platform you use."**

## Who we talk to

Operators of contact-centre quality. Specifically:

- **Contact Centre Managers** — running 5–250-seat operations,
  responsible for volume and quality at a glance.
- **QA Managers / Analysts** — own the scorecards, rule failure
  patterns, review queue. Care most about coverage and consistency.
- **Compliance Teams** — own audit, regulatory, and risk surfaces.
  Care most about evidence and complete coverage of recorded
  interactions.
- **Operations Directors** — sign the cheque, want margin-defensible
  pricing and proof the tool reduced QA review time.
- **Team Leaders** — front-line supervisors needing coaching prompts
  with evidence, not vibes.

NOT our buyer: C-suite "digital transformation" narratives, enterprise
RFP committees buying a £40-£160/seat platform, agents themselves
(no self-service in v1).

## How we sound

**Voice register:** Pragmatic operator. Closer to a senior consultant
who has run a QA floor than to a SaaS marketing page. Specific over
inspirational. Numbers over adjectives.

**The five rules** (in priority order — when two conflict, lower
number wins):

1. **Lead with the operational problem, not the product feature.**
   "Manual QA covers 2–5% of calls" before "AI-powered scoring."
2. **Quote prices and units of work openly.** £4.99/seat, 40 pooled
   hours, £0.50/hour overage. No "contact us for pricing."
3. **Name the boundary.** Insights360 does not record calls, does not
   run in real-time, does not give agents a self-service portal in v1.
   Boundaries build trust.
4. **No transformation language.** We do not "revolutionise,"
   "empower," or "unlock potential." We score calls and surface risk.
5. **Short sentences when the claim is concrete. Longer sentences
   only when explaining how a system works.**

## Canonical figures (use verbatim — do not improvise)

These pin the recurring claims so every asset agrees. When an example
or figure is needed, use THESE — never invent an alternative.

- **The QA-coverage worked example is always the 30-seat broker:** a
  30-seat broker handles **~8,000 calls a month**; a 2-person QA team
  reviews **160–400** of them (the **2–5%** industry standard); the
  other **~7,600 go unreviewed**. (ContactBabel 2024, via the the research team
  pack.) Do NOT switch to a 40-/50-/100-seat example mid-campaign —
  one house figure across the set. (The "5% of calls at 100 seats"
  MaxContact hook quote is a *verbatim external quote*, a separate
  thing from our worked example — quote it as theirs, don't blend it.)
- **Pricing:** £4.99/seat/month, 40 pooled hours, £0.50/hr overage,
  £1,280 setup. No 50-seat minimum.
- **Telephony — compatibility vs automated feed:** claim
  *compatibility* with any listed platform freely ("compatible with /
  works with recordings from X"). Only **3CX and OAK (ClarifyGo)** have
  an **automated feed**; never imply a seamless/automated/connected
  integration for the others (they are "scoped per customer during
  onboarding"). See integrations.md § compatible-vs-wired.

## Words we never use

Generic SaaS vendor language that contradicts the precise, evidence-led
register. Banned outright:

- **"leverage"** — say "use".
- **"synergy" / "synergistic"** — meaningless.
- **"holistic"** — vague; name the actual coverage.
- **"end-to-end solution"** — Insights360 is explicitly NOT end-to-end
  (we don't record; we don't run in real-time). Saying this is a lie.
- **"ecosystem"** — overused. Say "integrations" if that's what you
  mean.
- **"best-in-class" / "world-class" / "next-generation"** — empty
  positioning. Name the specific advantage instead.
- **"seamlessly"** — banned *unless* directly describing the Microsoft
  Teams integration (where it's load-bearing — Teams users genuinely
  expect it). Never use it elsewhere.
- **"revolutionise" / "transformation" / "journey"** — transformation
  language is Rule 4 territory.
- **"unlock" / "unleash" / "empower"** — see Rule 4.
- **"cutting-edge" / "innovative" / "disruptive"** — adjectives in
  place of a claim.
- **"AI-powered"** — reserved for decorative product-positioning use
  *only* (e.g., a product-page hero). Never use in body copy, briefs,
  outbound, or anywhere the claim is doing real work. Replace with
  what it actually does: "Bedrock Claude Haiku scores the transcript".

## Examples

### Good copy

> "Score 100% of calls instead of 2%. £4.99/seat per month, 40 pooled
> hours of analysis. No 50-seat minimum, no annual lock-in."

> "Insights360 runs after the call — never during. Recordings come
> from your existing telephony (3CX, Teams, Cisco). We score them
> against your rules, flag compliance risk, and surface trends to
> your supervisors. Your call data never leaves UK Vercel + AWS."

> "Manual QA covers 2–5% of your calls. The other 95% — including
> every escalation, every complaint, every compliance-adjacent
> conversation — is invisible. Insights360 is the audit trail for
> those calls."

### Bad copy (and why)

> ~~"Revolutionise your contact centre with AI-powered insights that
> empower your agents to deliver world-class customer experiences."~~
> Violates Rules 1, 4, and 5. Empty. Lists no number, no boundary,
> no specific problem. Could be any SaaS.

> ~~"Our holistic, end-to-end conversational AI ecosystem seamlessly
> unlocks the full potential of your customer interactions."~~
> Six banned words in one sentence. Also factually wrong —
> Insights360 is not end-to-end (no recording, no real-time).

> ~~"Real-time call analytics for the modern contact centre."~~
> Insights360 is NOT real-time. This is a lie. Use "post-call" or
> "near-immediate after call ends" instead.

## Conversion conventions

CTAs must come from this fixed set — match the website exactly:

- **"Book a demo"** — primary CTA on product pages. Direct, professional.
- **"Set up a free trial"** — for buyers ready to evaluate. The trial
  is a real product trial, not a sandbox.
- **"Arrange a demo"** — variant of the above for outbound and
  long-form content. Slightly more formal.
- **"Try interactive demo"** — for content where the buyer can self-serve
  a guided product walkthrough without a sales call.

**Never use:**
- "Start for free!" (consumer SaaS tone)
- "Sign up now" (transactional, low trust)
- "Get started in seconds" (overpromise — Insights360 has a £1,280
  setup fee and a config review at month 1)
- Any exclamation marks in CTAs.

**Button copy register:** Professional, direct, low-pressure. The
buyer is making a six-month decision, not impulse-buying. Pre-CTA copy
should explain what happens next ("A 30-minute call to map your
telephony and walk you through a live tenant.") rather than create
urgency ("Don't miss out!").

**Error and empty-state copy** (carries the same voice):
- ✅ "Not enough history yet" — current product copy. Honest, specific.
- ✅ "No data available — there's nothing to display at the moment." —
  current product copy. Calm.
- ❌ "Oops! Something went wrong!" — exclamation, cartoonish.
- ❌ "Houston, we have a problem!" — banned. Always.

Insights360 inherits Conversant's brand identity. These are
documented as constraints, not editable:

- Grey: `#DCE6F0`

When the campaign team briefs the content team on visual assets, these are the constraints
to enforce.

## Notes for the campaign team

- **When ambiguous, prefer concrete operator-language angles over
  strategic-buyer angles.** The buyer is operational, not C-suite.
- **When two rules conflict, "Be specific" beats "Be brief."** A
  longer sentence that names a number or a boundary is better than a
  short one that doesn't.
- **The System A / System B distinction is a useful voice test.** Copy
  that respects it (post-call, pre-computed, no real-time) sounds like
  Insights360. Copy that implies real-time inference or end-to-end
  scope sounds wrong.
- **The headline differentiator is coverage, not price.** The
  100%-vs-2–5% claim leads. Price (£4.99/seat) is quoted openly in
  website/content, but in cold outbound sequences it is an offer-stage
  detail (Touch 3 only), and competitors are never named — differentiate
  against the status quo. See `outbound-craft.md` for outbound sequencing
  rules (single source).
- **The 100%-vs-2–5% coverage claim is the headline statistic** for
  most archetypes. Lead with it when the archetype's pain is "we don't
  know what we're missing."
- **For compliance-led campaigns**, the boundary statements ("post-call,
  pre-computed, never real-time") are trust-building, not limitations
  — name them up front.
- **For Coaching Notes briefs**, flag that the inline editor is in
  backlog (Theme 3 of the v2 roadmap). The read-only output is live;
  the content team should not imply Team Leaders can author coaching notes inline
  yet.
